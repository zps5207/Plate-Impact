Flat v2 Contact Workarounds

Eight deck variants of the flat_v2 mesher output · ROAR, 13 Oct 2026 · same analysis as the full diagnostic

A node-based contact surface on the plate works. With it, all 802 ghosts within 10 mm of the axis are deleted and there are zero islands (baseline: 58). A ball node was inside a live ghost element at most 1 time, against 490 in the baseline. Contact reached 1,087 plate nodes against 285, and the ball lost 9.1% of its kinetic energy against 1.9%.

The other ideas did not help: the whole-ghost surface changed nothing, and a linear penalty stiffness made things no better or worse.

What each idea did

  1. Whole-ghost contact surface (hypothesis H2): rejected. Giving Surf-1 every ghost element in place of the original exterior faces produced a result identical to the last digit from the baseline (energies, 1357 deleted ghosts, 285 contact nodes, 490 peak penetrating nodes). Abaqus evidently already extends the element-based surface to faces exposed by deletion, so the missing interior faces were not the problem.
  2. Linear penalty stiffness: no help. With 1e10 Pa/m the ball lost 0.2% of its energy and 976 of its nodes were inside live ghosts at once, worse than baseline. At 1e12 Pa/m it was close to baseline (1.2% lost, 636 nodes). The plate's internal energy stayed near zero, so it was barely loaded. I do not know why the stiffer setting does not help; the penalty may be limited by the stable time step or by the surface the ball meets.
  3. Node-based plate surface: works. Adding the whole-ghost surface changed nothing, which matches the finding above. The contact now acts on the plate's nodes, which carry the VU1 mass, so it no longer depends on the 1e-4 ghost. The ball slows from 1000 to 924 m/s, and passes through the plate by about 14 µs.
  4. Ghost scale 1e-2: no help (hypothesis H1 rejected). Making the ghost 100 times stiffer (E 50.8 kPa) gave 2.0% energy loss, ball exit speed 982 m/s, 484 peak penetrating nodes and 67 islands, essentially the baseline. So the soft ghost's penalty stiffness is not what lets the ball through.
  5. Penalty scale 1e4: diverged. The kinetic energy blew up (4.1 kJ to about 46 kJ by 13 µs) as the stable step fell to about 5e-12 s. It is not a result, and shows a stiffer penalty is not a usable route.

  • Within 10 mm of the axis everything is deleted: 130, 390 and 282 of the hosts in the three inner bands.
  • Damage beyond 10 mm roughly triples: 4946 ghosts deleted in total against 1357. The extra deletions are in the outer rings (1,266 at 15 to 25 mm, 1,977 at 25 to 60 mm, against 106 and 242). That is consistent with the fibers carrying load outward once the ball is actually stopped, the delamination-like effect you saw earlier, but I have not verified that.
  • VU1 and ghost still disagree for some hosts: 500 have a failed VU1 and a live ghost, and 482 ghosts were deleted with the VU1 intact. The hosts with a failed VU1 and a live ghost are all beyond 10 mm.

Cautions

  • Contact pressure is spiky. Peak CPRESS on plate nodes reaches 2e11 to 8e11 Pa, as expected for point contact (agy flagged this). The run completed with the same stable time step as the baseline and the same runtime (about 30 minutes), so it is stable, but the loads are impulsive.
  • The ball still goes through. It loses 9.1% of its energy and keeps 924 m/s. Whether that is physical for a steel ball at 1000 m/s into a 5 MPa plate 10 mm thick is a question for you; I cannot judge it from this model alone.
  • Gaps between nodes. The node surface uses an assigned thickness of 0.5 mm (half an element). I did not run a node surface without thickness, so I cannot say how much the thickness contributes.
  • Leftover nodes. Because VU1 elements are never deleted, plate nodes stay in contact after the host fails. The ball is long gone in this run, but a rebound or debris would meet them.
  • Ball erosion not visible. The ball STATUS output did not reach the ODB, so I cannot say whether ball elements eroded.

What I changed and what is open

  • Keyword fixes to agy's recipes (found by Abaqus datacheck): *Surface Property Assignment must sit inside *Contact, and *Contact Controls does not take a name, so I used *Contact Controls Assignment, type=SCALE PENALTY with the factor as its data.
  • Mesher: not yet changed. The node surface was added by editing the output deck (verification/roar/make_contact_variants.py). To make it automatic, the mesher would write *Surface, type=NODE over the plate nodes and the contact inclusion and thickness lines. I would do that next, if you want it.
  • Next test: a node surface without thickness, and with thicker values, to see what the thickness does; and a longer step to see the full crater.
