Concave curved plate against the flat plate

Ball speed. At 100 us the ball is moving at 457.8 m/s on the curved plate and 453.3 m/s on the flat plate, so the curved plate leaves it 4.6 m/s faster (91.6% of the 500 m/s retained against 90.7%). This is the mean velocity of the ball's nodes along the impact axis.

Energy. The ball loses 165.4 J of kinetic energy on the curved plate and 182.4 J on the flat plate (of 1,023.4 J). The model as a whole loses 42.2 J against 49.5 J, measured as the initial total energy minus Abaqus's ETOTAL at 100 us. Viscous dissipation is 14.8 J against 17.7 J. In every measure the curved plate takes less energy out of the ball, by 9% on ball kinetic energy.

Damage. The curved plate has 6,588 host elements deleted at 100 us against 3,548 for the flat plate, so it loses more material and still takes less energy from the ball. This is one run each, and the two plates also differ in fiber layout, so I would not read a mechanism from it without more cases.

Caveats

  • The plates are not identical apart from curvature: the fiber layout follows the curved surface and the contact thickness values differ slightly (table above), so some of the difference may come from those rather than the shape.
  • The model-energy figure is a bookkeeping difference. Plastic dissipation (ALLPD) was not extracted for the curved run, so I cannot split it into plastic work and energy lost to element deletion.
  • The side-view (plate vertical) videos are framed for a flat plate, so the raised curved rim runs off the top of the frame.
  • One run per configuration; small differences in speed or energy would need more cases to establish.
