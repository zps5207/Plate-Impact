Stage 4 report: dead-state hygiene (2026-10-01)

Branch: vuel-plastic-failure. Two real bookkeeping gaps found and fixed in uel/VUEL_failure.for (not just verified -- these were genuine bugs left over from earlier stages), plus two multi-element tests run purely observationally, per the goal's own framing ("Report what happens to nodes whose elements have all failed").

Fixes made

  1. Rayleigh/bulk-viscosity dissipation kept accumulating for a failed element. ELIE/PLASTIC_DISSIP were already guarded by .NOT. ELEM_ALREADY_FAILED (from Stage 1), but DAMP_DISSIP/ BV_DISSIP were not: STRESS_VISC/STRESS_BULK are computed from the dead element's current strain rate (its nodes keep moving under their own inertia even after it dies), and without the guard that nonzero stress-times-strain-rate kept silently adding "dissipation" energy for an element whose actual force contribution (STRESS_TOTAL, and hence FINT) is already zero -- a real energy-bookkeeping error, exactly what this stage was looking for. Fixed by wrapping both accumulations in the same guard.
  2. dtimeStable was never relaxed for a failed element. A dead element still has mass and the file still computed its elastic-wave- speed-based stable increment from its original (pre-failure) material properties, meaning it would keep capping the analysis's global stable time increment (Abaqus takes the minimum over all elements) for the rest of the run, for no physical reason. Fixed: dtimeStable(KBLOCK) = 1.0D30 once ELEM_ALREADY_FAILED.

Kinetic energy (ELKE) was deliberately left untouched -- a dead element's nodes still have real mass and velocity, and that's a genuine, correct contribution to the model's total KE that should not disappear just because the element stopped exerting force. Mass itself was (already, unavoidably) left untouched -- amass is set once at jMassCalc and this file has no hook to revisit it.

Decision on stored elastic energy at the moment of failure, now documented in the file header: it is NOT preserved, redirected into a dissipation bucket, or otherwise accounted for -- ELIE (recomputed fresh from the current strain state every call, not accumulated) simply reads 0.0 the instant the element goes inert, since the guard that stops it accumulating starts on the very next call. This means the whole-model ETOTAL balance will show a one-increment discontinuous drop at the moment of failure, equal to whatever elastic strain energy was stored in that element the instant before. Confirmed directly below, not just asserted.

Deck 1: t10_damping_hygiene (confirms both fixes with real numbers)

Single VU1 hex, same uniaxial-stress BC as T6-T9, but with nonzero BETA_RAYLEIGH=1e-6 and bulk viscosity (b1=0.06, b2=1.2) -- every earlier stage zeroed these to keep comparisons clean; here the point is to have real damping active so its bookkeeping can be checked. Driven to tension past EPS_FAIL=0.02. Trips via *** VUEL_FAILURE (ENG STRAIN): elem = 1 time = 5.0087031E-03.

t (s) ALLIE (J) ALLVD (J)
0.004873 3.6986e7 2.1931e4

ALLIE drops to exactly 0.0 the frame after the trip and stays there; ALLVD freezes at exactly 2.351392e4 -- bit-for-bit identical at every subsequent output frame through the end of the run, confirming the dissipation-accumulation fix works exactly as intended (before the fix, with nonzero damping and a still-moving dead element, ALLVD would have kept climbing). ETOTAL shows the corresponding large one-increment drop (from -2.9e2 to -4.3e7) at that same increment, then stays essentially flat (drifting by ~1e2 absolute, i.e. ~0.0003%, over the remaining 5 ms -- consistent with ordinary KE evolution, not further energy leakage) -- directly confirming the documented "stored elastic energy vanishes at the instant of failure, nothing else is injected or leaked afterward" design.

Deck 2: t10_two_element -- genuinely orphaned node, free-flight

Two identical VU1 hexes in series along x (sharing a 4-node interface face), same EPS_FAIL=0.02 for both, end-to-end tension driven from the far end. Two equal segments in series split an imposed displacement evenly, so both reach the threshold in the same increment -- confirmed:

*** VUEL_FAILURE (ENG STRAIN): elem = 2  time = 4.4935420E-03

The driven far-end node, by contrast, continues decelerating smoothly toward its prescribed final displacement (kinematically controlled by the *Boundary condition, unaffected by either element's failure).

Deck 3: t10_three_chain -- cascading failure observation (per the

goal, reported descriptively, not pass/fail)

Three identical-material VU1 hexes in series, staggered EPS_FAIL (elem1=0.015, elem2=0.025, elem3=0.04) so they are intended to fail in sequence as the far end is driven further. Only elem1 ever trips (t=2.9956952E-03); elem2 and elem3 never reach their thresholds in this 0.01 s step, despite the imposed end displacement (0.3 over length 3) being large enough that, if the structure behaved quasi- statically, elem2 and elem3 would also have been expected to fail.

What actually happens, from the interface nodes' U1/V1 history: once elem1 dies, the interface node between elem1 and elem2 (node 2) is no longer connected to the fixed wall through any live stiffness path (elem1 is dead; the only live connection left is through elem2 toward the still-driven far end). With zero damping (deliberately, same as every earlier stage's isolation decks), this un-anchored node goes into violent, undamped oscillation rather than settling into a new quasi-static state: V1 at node 2 swings from values near 10-25 m/s (during the still-elastic early loading) to peaks over 150 m/s and troughs below -90 m/s within a few tenths of a millisecond after elem1's death, and is still oscillating with tens-of-m/s amplitude at the end of the run (V1=40.5 m/s at t=0.01, still moving). U1 at the same node does not monotonically track the imposed end displacement the way it would in a quasi-static picture -- it overshoots past 0.14, drops back to 0.117, and ends the run at 0.328 (vs. a naive quasi-static expectation of roughly 1/3 of 0.3 = 0.1 once two segments remain), driven by this ringing rather than by steadily increasing strain. Neither elem2 nor elem3's own engineering strain ever climbs high enough, because the energy that would have gone into monotonic stretching instead went into this oscillation. No NaN, no abort, rc=0, THE ANALYSIS HAS COMPLETED SUCCESSFULLY -- the simulation itself stays numerically well-behaved throughout, but the intended "cascading failure" narrative does not play out the way a quasi-static mental model would predict.

Takeaway for the real plate-impact use case: killing one element in a load path does not simply "redistribute load smoothly to the next weakest link." With zero damping, it can instead destabilize the remaining connected structure into large-amplitude dynamic ringing, which may or may not ever revisit the strain levels needed to propagate failure further, depending entirely on the dynamics of what's left. This is reported as an observed fact about the current (zero-damping, immediate-zero-force) dead-state design, not a defect -- but it's a strong signal that Stage 5's "ramped force degradation" option (smoothing the transition instead of an instantaneous drop to zero) could matter a great deal for whether cascading failure propagates realistically, and that realistic (nonzero) damping is probably necessary for any multi- element VUEL-chain simulation to behave sensibly after the first failure.

Open issues

  • The t10_three_chain result above means Stage 4's "small multi-element test to see cascading failure" did not actually observe cascading in this particular configuration -- re-running with nonzero damping, or a slower/longer loading ramp, might produce a cleaner cascading sequence, but was not attempted here since the un-cascaded result is itself the more important, honestly-reported finding for this stage.
  • No node in t10_three_chain became fully orphaned (would require BOTH of a node's flanking elements to fail; only elem1 ever did), so this deck did not exercise the fully-orphaned-node case -- t10_two_element covers that cleanly instead.

Next: Stage 5

Only after this (Stage 1-4) baseline is accepted: ramped force degradation before the dead state, and an optional compression-only residual (volumetric stiffness only) for failed elements, each behind its own prop flag, compared against this stage's zero-rhs baseline -- directly motivated by the t10_three_chain finding above.
