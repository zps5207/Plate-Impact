Vals ball-failure tests (bt1): six 12 µs variants against vp5

Each deck changes one feature of the full vp5 native Vals deck and runs 12 µs (8 tasks, 32 GB, via run.sh). vp5 is the baseline, cut to 12 µs, because b0_base was never run. ROAR directory ctv/bt1.

Correction (2026-10-28). The “ball hexes inverted” figures in this report were computed from node positions over all ball hexes, including elements the solver had already deleted. Matching per-element STATUS from the ODBs (b4_d4fix and c2_truss_d4_md80 at 1000 m/s) shows 609 of 610 and 618 of 619 inverted hexes were already deleted when they first inverted, and 0–1 live ball hexes are inverted at 12 µs. So the inversion column measures the leftover nodes of deleted elements, not live element failure. The 12 µs ball-deletion counts are unaffected. Also, ODB energies were mislabelled 1000 times too small in the first version of this report (the ODB is in J; the ball carries 3.55 kJ); this is fixed. Details: verification/roar/ball_tests/t051/inv_alive_analysis.py.

Analysis

  • No variant changes the energy picture. Over 12 µs ALLKE never drops below 97.7% of its start and ETOTAL ends within 0.3% of its start in all seven runs. The energy problem seen in vp4 is not present at this time scale in any deck.
  • Ball erosion starts at 1.5–1.9 µs and runs at a steady 80–90 elements per µs in every run. By 12 µs that is 4–5% of the ball (b6 316L is lowest at 3.9%, b1 highest at 5.1%). None of the six changes stops it. The spread between variants is small compared with the erosion itself.
  • The D4 change (b4) removes about 8% of the ball deletions (840 against vp5's 913) but not the erosion, so negative D4 is not the main reason the ball is eroding. The two steel cards (b5, b6) give 850 and 721 deletions; b6 deletes the least ball, and it is also the only deck with a different density.
  • Beam fibers (b1, b3) delete more fibers and delete them earlier: first fiber deletion at 5.3–6.3 µs against 8.75 µs in vp5, and 41 and 24 fibers deleted against 7. The T3D2 decks give 2–14. I have not established why. vp5 was sampled every ~1.75 µs, so its first-deletion times are coarse; other decks were sampled every ~0.3 µs.
  • Interface damage tracks the material changes. Points past half damage at 12 µs: b4 941, b6 884, b5 857, b1 829, b3 771, vp5 767, b2 717. Points fully failed are few (5–26) and highest for the beam-fiber decks (b1 26, b3 24).
  • The new ball mesh (b2) is the cheap change. It finished in 1 h 19 min, against 3 h 50 min for b4 (which differs from vp5 only in D4) and 4 h 11 min for b1. The stable time increment I read from the solver status files supports this: b2 was ball-limited at 2.6×10-8 s (frame at 3.7 µs), against 5–6×10-9 s for b1 and b4 at 1–1.5 µs; b3's critical element was in a fiber ply at 1.0×10-8 s (frame at 2.1–10 µs). b2 also has the lowest interface damage and the fewest fiber deletions. Its ball has 17,248 elements against 18,500, so ball deletion counts are not directly comparable by number; the fractions are 4.6% against 4.9% for vp5.
  • Ball z-velocity should not be over-read. It is the median over ball nodes and the ball is shredded; values run from −888 (b5) to −1000 m/s.

Limits: one run per variant, 12 µs only, no b0_base, element counts sampled at the output frames, and no run-to-run repeat to separate small differences from noise.
