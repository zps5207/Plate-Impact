Flat plate. At 100 us the ball is moving at 457.8 m/s on the plywise plate and 453.3 m/s on the solid plate (+4.5 m/s). The ball loses 165.6 J of kinetic energy on the plywise plate against 182.4 J on the solid plate, and 11.7% of the plywise host is deleted against 14.8% of the solid host.

Concave plate. At 100 us the ball is moving at 456.2 m/s on the plywise plate and 457.8 m/s on the solid plate (-1.6 m/s). The ball loses 171.5 J of kinetic energy on the plywise plate against 165.4 J on the solid plate, and 12.2% of the plywise host is deleted against 27.5% of the solid host.

Caveats

  • Penetration is measured on the exported nodes against the convex hull of the exported ball nodes (the same test as the earlier fiber-contact report). The ball is nearly convex, but a surface dent would make the depth slightly too large. Host nodes with no surviving element are excluded from the main host count, since Abaqus drops them from the node contact, and are listed separately in the table.
  • This is a visual and capability check. One run per configuration, no repeat.
  • Plywise has 8 plies of 1.25 mm and the solid host has 10 layers of 1 mm, so the element counts and the element height differ; the fraction of the host deleted is the fair comparison, not the count.
  • Fiber contact thickness is the plate value in the plywise decks (0.496 mm flat, 0.499 mm concave); the solid runs used 0.471 to 0.473 mm.
  • Some host cells carry a computed fiber volume fraction at or above 1 (about 17 to 21 per ply); the VUEL floors the matrix fraction there. A 1 mm fiber in a 1.25 mm ply is at the 0.8x guidance limit.
  • Abaqus reports the cohesive interfaces as accepted; damage output of the interfaces (CSDMG) was not requested, so delamination is judged from the videos only.
