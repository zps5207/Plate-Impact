What had to change in the mesher

  1. Parser. embmesh/parser.py crashed on *Surface, type=ELEMENT lines because it expected an element number. It now accepts an element-set name too.
  2. Surface remap. Surf-1 pointed at elements 1 to 22400. In vuel-ghost mode those are user elements, which have no contact faces. embmesh/vuelghost.py now moves the members of any element-based surface set onto the matching ghost labels (24001 and up). After the fix the sets span 24001 to 48000.

The 46 existing tests pass. The mesher output for this deck is in Flat-vuel-ghost-v2/.

Deck points to know about

  • The deck has no node-based surfaces. The ball and plate node sets exist but contact does not use them.
  • The ball starts 1 mm from the plate, down from 10 mm in the earlier decks, so impact begins almost immediately.
  • Contact is no longer ALL EXTERIOR. Whether faces exposed by deletion join a fixed element-based surface is not confirmed. It is what this run is testing.

Surviving islands

An island is a ghost that is still alive at the end, within 10 mm of the axis, and has at least 60% of its neighbours deleted (3 or more neighbours within 1.6 element pitches). There are {{N_ISL}}. They sit in the middle of the thickness and the outer part of the crater, none in the top or bottom two layers.

The 10 mm limit is a property of the check, not of the plate. The radius distribution may continue outwards, and I have not looked beyond 10 mm.

Plastic strain of the survivors

The ghosts delete when their equivalent plastic strain (PEEQ) reaches the ductile-initiation value, 1e-6. PEEQ never decreases, so a value of zero at the end means it was zero throughout. All {{N_PEEQ_ZERO}} of the {{N_ALIVE_NEAR}} living near-axis ghosts read exactly zero, including the islands.

What that means. The island ghosts did not fail because they never yielded in the ghost's own J2 plasticity. Their neighbours were deleted, so these elements were not strained enough to trip the ghost criterion, whatever happened to the VUEL elements sharing their nodes.

Possible explanations, none yet tested:

  • The VUEL fails on a normal-strain measure that responds to compression, while the ghost's J2 plasticity ignores pure hydrostatic compression. An element squeezed almost isotropically would fail the VUEL test but never yield in the ghost.
  • The elements move with the debris as a nearly rigid body once their neighbours are gone, so they carry no strain.

Two limits on this result. The PEEQ values were sampled every 10th output frame plus the last, so a short transient between samples would be missed (it cannot explain a final value of zero). And the deleted elements read zero PEEQ after deletion, so I cannot compare the survivors with the elements that failed.

Do the survivors move? Island history

To see what the island ghosts were doing, I extracted per-frame histories (every 10th frame, 101 samples from 0 to 70 µs) for the {{N_ISL}} islands and for {{N_ADJ}} deleted ghosts next to them. The quantity is the displacement of the element centroid.

The islands barely moved. Their peak centroid displacement has a median of {{ISL_MED}} mm (largest {{ISL_MAX}} mm). The deleted ghosts beside them have a median of {{DEAD_MED}} mm (largest {{DEAD_MAX}} mm). That is about {{RATIO}} times less motion.

The islands stayed elastic: the largest Mises stress seen in any island ghost is {{ISL_MISES}}, below the ghost yield of about 61.1 (same deck units), which is why PEEQ stayed at zero.

Where the ball goes

The ball is a sphere of radius about 10 mm (its mesh reaches r = 9.99 mm and spans 20 mm in z). It passes the whole plate thickness, so anything within r of about 10 mm of the axis should be inside its swept volume. Of the {{N_ISL}} islands, {{N_R9}} sit at r below 9 mm and {{N_R8}} below 8 mm. The ball went through them without moving them.

Reading. This points at contact rather than at the failure criteria. If the island ghosts had carried contact pressure, the ball would have pushed them, they would have yielded or been carried down, and the ghost criterion would have fired. They show no sign of being touched.

This is a hypothesis, not a test. The earlier runs with ALL EXTERIOR contact also left islands, so the surface-pair change did not cure it. I have not yet looked at contact pressure at the island nodes, or at how far ball nodes pass into live plate material. The VU1 elements under the islands could be alive or failed, which is still unknown.

Checks that would settle it:

  • Contact output at the islands. CPRESS at the island nodes. Zero throughout means they were never in contact.
  • Ball-node penetration. Distance ball nodes travel inside live plate material at the last frame.
  • Node-based surface. Put the plate nodes in a node-based contact surface so interior nodes exposed by deletion stay in contact.

Two independent investigations of this run are running (a physics angle and a code-and-Abaqus-rules angle). Their results are not in this report.

Open items

  1. VU1 failure flag. The ODB has no SDV fields. The request for SDV49, SDV106 and SDV107 was accepted by Abaqus but nothing was written, so a user element's state variables cannot be read this way. Whether the island VU1 elements are alive or dead is still unknown. Candidates: reading the VUEL's stdout (its element labels are scrambled by concurrent writes) or a different way of exporting the state.
  2. Contact method. The island motion data points at contact (see above). This run does not say whether the surface-pair contact fixed anything. A node-based surface on the plate nodes is the next option if you want to try nodal contact.
  3. Criterion mismatch. If the VU1 elements around the islands are dead, the VUEL and ghost criteria disagree on compressive loading. Changing either criterion changes the physics and needs your decision first.
  4. Housekeeping. T-004, T-006, the diagnostic patch and the two code changes above are still uncommitted.
