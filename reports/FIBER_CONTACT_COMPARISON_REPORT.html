Fiber contact: v500 against v500*

500 m/s ball impact, 0 to 100 us · ROAR jobs 56489085 and 56489086 · branch vuel-plastic-failure · 16 Oct 2026

Result. Putting the fiber nodes into the node-based ball contact stops the fibers passing through the ball. The peak number of fiber nodes inside the ball falls from 519 to 5, and the deepest penetration from 3.80 mm to 0.17 mm. The plate nodes, which were already in contact, stay outside the ball in both runs (the control).

Side effects. With fiber contact 3,548 host elements are deleted by 100 us against 2,414 without it, and the ball ends at 453 m/s against 465 m/s. The two deletion curves are the same up to about 20 us and then separate, and the gap keeps growing through 100 us. The fibers now carry load against the ball, which changes how much of the plate fails and how much speed the ball keeps. These are one run per case; the size of the differences is not tested against mesh or setup changes.

Fibers. In the wireframe videos the fibers no longer sit inside the ball. They are held at its surface and thrown outward as it passes. At 100 us 818 fiber nodes are below the plate's lower face against 321, and 50 segments are stretched by more than 10% against 4. The largest segment strain is 66 against 25 (engineering strain: 66 means a segment stretched to 67 times its original length). I did not check whether the fiber elements have a failure criterion; strains this large suggest they do not break.

Caveats

  • Penetration is measured on the exported nodes against the convex hull of the ball. The ball is nearly convex, but a dent in its surface would make the depth slightly too large. Host nodes are the control: they reach at most 0.29 mm (v500) and 0.22 mm (v500*) inside the hull, which is the noise floor of this test.
  • Both runs share the earlier finding that 821 outer-annulus host elements have a fiber volume fraction of 1 or more and therefore almost no mass or stiffness. The corrected volume fractions are not in either run.
  • The fiber contact thickness (0.471 mm) is the mesher's automatic choice and was not tuned. Abaqus may let contact take precedence over the embedded constraint for these nodes, so the fibers' response to the host could differ from the no-contact case for reasons other than the contact force.
  • One run per configuration. Differences of a few percent in deletions or ball speed would need more cases (speeds, meshes) to establish.
