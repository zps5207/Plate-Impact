## Context
Native 20-ply Vals quarter-disc deck (1000 m/s steel ball, 70 us). vp4 = node-based ball contact on every host node (0.635 mm thickness): energy blow-up. vp5 = same deck but the ball contacts the host through an eroding element-based surface `EMBMESH_PLATE_ELEMS`; ALLKE now only +6.8% over the 3.55 J start (job 56651971, 2h23, OK). But the ball erodes badly in vp5: 3,161 of 18,500 ball elements deleted (17%), survivors stretched into long spikes, ball median z-velocity stays -1000 m/s (plate barely slows it), 1,351/80,500 hosts and 70/206,930 fibers deleted. A steel ball failing this way at 1000 m/s on Dyneema is not physical; something overloads it.
Ball material "Mild Steel": Johnson-Cook damage (`*Damage Initiation criterion=JOHNSON COOK`, `*Damage Evolution DISPLACEMENT 0.`), section controls `element deletion=YES`.
vp6 = vp5 with the ball's JC damage removed (ball cannot erode). It ran 1h45 then died at 65.6 us: "Floating Point Exception in increment 18397", "Excessive distortion" of BALL-1 elements 509, 475, 502, 479. A special output frame was written at 65.64 us, so the ODB has frames up to there.
Ball contacts: ball = element surface `EMBMESH_BALL_SURF`; partners are `EMBMESH_PLATE_ELEMS` (eroding element surface of all 20 plies) and `EMBMESH_FIBER_NODES` (fiber nodes, THICKNESS 0.618 mm). Plies are tied with cohesive general contact (K 60 N/mm3, 1.8 MPa). Units are SI; ODB energy histories are in mJ.
