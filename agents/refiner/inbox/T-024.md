## What the user wants
The user said: this is a smoke test purely for curved geometry with everything else the same; once the mesher can handle curved geometry, ply-wise comes next. So: no plies, no cohesive, no per-ply host elsets, no one-element-thick deck. Input is a 10-layer curved HOST-ONLY deck (24,000 hosts; see item 0). The mesher must produce, through its own pipeline and writers, the same kind of output deck it makes today for the flat plate (single host elset `EMBMESH_HOST`, `EMBMESH_FIBERS_T0/T90`, same embedding block, plate-node + fiber-node contact, same materials/step/BCs, ball -500 m/s, d = 1 mm class fibers). The only difference is that fiber layers follow the curved plate.

## Why the current mesher cannot do it
`--curved flat` lays each fiber layer in a flat plane at a constant coordinate along the thickness axis (`embmesh/fibers.py: flat_disc_fibers`). On a plate whose surface sags by up to 5 mm with a 10 mm thickness, those planes leave the plate. `cylindrical_fibers` / `spherical_fibers` are different layup families (axis or center based) and are not what we want here. We want the same layer structure (rows at pitch d+gap, layers stacked through the thickness, direction alternating layer to layer, ends inset d/2, clipped to the host outline), but every row is a polyline that FOLLOWS THE SURFACE.

## Geometry model (agreed with the user)
The curved plate is a translation stack: back face = front face translated by one stack vector; the surface shape is arbitrary but identical front and back. Fibers run along the two world axes other than the stack axis (0 and 90), straight in projection along the stack axis, pitch measured in the projected plane (not along the surface), height follows the surface. A fiber layer at offset t above the reference (bottom) face lies on z = f(x,y) + t along the stack axis.

Verify your host-only deck is such a plate (a warp of Flatv2 by a spherical sag, R = 0.25 m, max slope 11.47 deg). The original Flatv2 faces differ by up to 76 um, but your rebuilt host-only deck is an exact translate; still keep the congruence tolerance in the mesher for real decks, take the bottom face as the reference surface, and report the mismatch.

Layer placement (decided by the user): fiber layers are spaced `d + gap` apart ALONG THE STACK AXIS, exactly as the flat layup does, so the layer count equals the flat case (10 layers for d = 1 mm in 10 mm). On the slope the normal spacing between layers is (d+gap)*cos(theta), so neighbouring fibers may overlap by up to 1 - cos(theta_max) (about 2% at 11.5 deg). That is accepted: embedded truss elements are independent lines with no fiber-to-fiber contact, only each fiber's nodes must lie inside a host hex. Do NOT shrink d or reduce the layer count. Report theta_max, the maximum overlap fraction, the layer count, and the volume-fraction change versus a flat plate. For a flat plate the output must be IDENTICAL to today's flat layup.
