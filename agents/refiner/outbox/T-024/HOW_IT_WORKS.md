# Curved translation stack

`build_deck.py --no-fibers` reuses T-023's `load_base`, `stack_length`, and `stack`. The source has one curved top quad mesh and a bottom face. `stack_length` averages corresponding top to bottom node displacements and rejects a nontranslation. `stack` rebuilds eleven shared node planes at ten equal offsets along that vector, producing 24,000 C3D8R hosts. `make_one_element_deck.py` in T-023 had already rebuilt the source bottom as an exact translate. Its source-face mismatch was 76.42 μm maximum (4.53 μm mean, 15.99 μm 95th percentile); the accepted tolerance was 100 μm, or 10.64% of the median 0.940 mm in-plane edge. The rebuilt host faces differ only by floating-point roundoff.

The T-022/T-023 fiber scripts projected the convex C-mesh quads onto the two in-plane axes. For a row, they intersected each quad edge with the scanline, then interpolated height between the two crossings. Adjacent covered intervals were joined into a continuous row. T-023 inset each end until its projected distance to the exterior mesh edges reached `d/2`. Crossing vertices were retained at node lines; when a segment was shorter than 5% of its local in-plane edge, the near crossing was merged onto the adjacent node line by deleting the short interval. The remaining vertices and segment samples were checked for host containment.

The new `stacked_surface_fibers` applies that geometry in world coordinates. It finds the stack direction from each hex's paired faces, identifies the reference face by face adjacency, checks face translation mismatch against `max(10 nm, 11% of median edge)`, and measures the slope from bilinear quad derivatives. Each projected scanline intersects reference quads; crossing heights come from linear interpolation on the quad edge. Fibers are placed at offsets from the reference surface spaced by `d+gap` along the stack axis. Each end is inset until its projected distance to the outline reaches `d/2`, then nudged inward by a tiny numerical margin. Near node-line crossings are merged as above; zero-length segments are dropped. The independent written-deck check tests every vertex and segment midpoint against world-frame hosts.

For a planar deck, `stacked` calls the existing `flat_disc_fibers` directly, preserving its exact output. This case is recognized from equally spaced thickness planes because the original Flatv2 hex connectivity does not pair congruent projected faces; its per-hex face translation mismatch is much larger than the curved translation-stack tolerance.

T-023's `check.py` missed its frame bug because it checked generated fiber coordinates and host coordinates in the same Plate **part** frame before writing. The Plate-1 instance has a rotation and translation, while EMBFIB-1 was written with no transform. Those coordinates no longer coincide in the assembly. The mesher convention here is to use `select_elements`, which applies the Plate-1 transform, compute fibers in world coordinates, and write EMBFIB-1 with no instance transform.
