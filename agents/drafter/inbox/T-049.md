## Context
Native 20-ply Vals quarter-disc deck (1000 m/s steel ball, 70 us, SI units). vp5 = ball contacts the host through an eroding element-based surface and the fibers through node contact (THICKNESS 0.618 mm); ALLKE conserved to +6.8%, but 3,161 of 18,500 ball elements (17%) are deleted by Johnson-Cook failure and the survivors stretch into spikes; the ball median velocity stays -1000 m/s. vp6 = same deck, ball JC damage removed: the ball elements distort until the solver dies (Floating Point Exception at 65.6 us; "Excessive distortion" of BALL-1 elements 509, 475, 502, 479). So the ball breaks either way; the question is what overloads it. Codex is analysing the extracted ODB data in parallel (T-050); you are the model-side / experiment-design half. Do not duplicate his extraction work.
Facts: Ball "Mild Steel": rho 7850, E 203 GPa, nu 0.33, JC plasticity (3.0433e8, 4.22e8, 0.345, 0.87, 1800, 293), JC damage D1..D5 = 0.1152, 1.0116, -1.7684, -0.05279, 0.5262, displacement evolution 0. Host matrix and fiber cards are in the deck. Contact: `*Contact Inclusions` = (`EMBMESH_PLATE_ELEMS`, `EMBMESH_BALL_SURF`), (`EMBMESH_FIBER_NODES`, `EMBMESH_BALL_SURF`), plus cohesive ply-to-ply pairs. `*Section Controls EMBMESH_DELETE_ALL element deletion=YES` on every section. Fibers are embedded T3D2 in hexes. Earlier finding (coupon tests): thick (0.635 mm) node contact on host nodes created energy; an eroding element-based host surface fixed it.

   - ball vs a rigid or plain elastic plate (no fibers, no cohesive): does the ball survive at all? (baseline for mesh/material)
   - ball vs host-only (element surface), with and without fibers embedded;

   - contact formulation alternatives for the ball against fibers (element-based fiber surface via beam/truss section thickness, or tied/penalty options), if Abaqus/Explicit allows.
