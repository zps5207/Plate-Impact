# T-052
role: drafter (agy)
round: 1
goal: Work out, from Abaqus/Explicit practice and the physics, why a steel ball's C3D8R hex elements invert at 1000 m/s on this laminate, and design a small test matrix of fixes to try (decks plus a prediction for each).

## Context
Native 20-ply Vals quarter-disc deck, 1000 m/s steel ball (18,500 elements: C3D8R plus 1,500 C3D6R wedges), Abaqus/Explicit, SI units. Across the bt1 and bt2 test series (12 us runs) the ball erodes from ~1.5-1.9 us and 1.5-3.9% of ball hexes have a non-positive corner Jacobian by 12 us; the user believes elements invert and blow up before they delete. Tried and not effective: beam vs truss fibers, JC damage D4 = +0.002 (instead of -0.05279), matrix max degradation 0.8, steel card variants (A, 316L), a uniform O-grid ball mesh (b2, 3x faster but same erosion). Ball: JC plasticity, JC damage with displacement evolution 0 (abrupt), `*Section Controls ... element deletion=YES`. The ball contacts the host through an eroding element surface `EMBMESH_PLATE_ELEMS` and the fibers through node contact `EMBMESH_FIBER_NODES` with THICKNESS 0.618 mm (3.7x the smallest ball element). Codex (T-051) is analysing the extracted data in parallel; you are the model-side and experiment-design half. Do not duplicate his analysis. Earlier work of yours: T-049 (`agents/drafter/outbox/T-049/`, coupon decks 01-10 and BALL_TEST_PLAN.md); reuse its generator where it helps.

## Deliverable (outbox/T-052/)
1. `INVERSION_FIXES.md`: the candidate mechanisms for hex inversion in Abaqus/Explicit under this loading (reduced-integration hourglassing, loss of support after neighbour deletion, contact-node intrusion, no distortion-based deletion, very large strain rate in an element with abrupt-fail damage, mass-scaling or time-increment interplay, anything else you can justify), and for each the Abaqus remedy, with the exact keyword and parameter (for example `*Section Controls` hourglass and `distortion control`, `second order accuracy`, `kinematic split`, `max degradation`, `element deletion` criteria, ALE adaptive meshing, converting failed elements to SPH or particles, a coarser or finer ball, a smoother contact definition). Say which of these the Abaqus version used on ROAR actually supports if you can verify it from the deck or docs, and what you are unsure of.
2. `make_inversion_tests.py` generating a SMALL matrix of decks (minutes on 8 CPUs; take the ball and a reduced plate from the bt2 or T-049 coupon setup, not the full 40 GB model), changing ONE ingredient at a time from the bt2 c2 truss + D4 reference. Suggested axes: hourglass control variants; distortion control on; ball sections with and without element deletion; ball mesh (current vs O-grid vs finer); fiber contact thickness 0.618 vs smaller; a rigid or elastic plate as a "ball alone" control.
3. `INVERSION_TEST_PLAN.md`: what each deck isolates, a written prediction for each before it runs, which result would point to which cause, and the run order. Metrics per deck: fraction of ball hexes with non-positive Jacobian over time (reuse `verification/roar/ball_tests/ball_inversion.py`), ball elements deleted, ALLKE/ETOTAL, run time.
4. Run datachecks (not solves) if allowed, and report their result.
