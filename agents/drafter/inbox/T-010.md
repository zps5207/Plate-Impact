# T-010
role: drafter
round: 1
goal: Brainstorm the best WORKAROUND so the ball is actually stopped by the plate in the VUEL + ghost scheme. The user suspects a nodal (node-to-node or node-to-surface) contact between plate nodes and ball nodes. Evaluate that and every alternative, rank them, and give a concrete deck/mesher recipe for the top two.

## Why this task exists

A full ODB diagnostic of flat_v2 (read `evidence/flat_v2_diagnostics_report.html` first, then the data files) shows the root problem is not the failure criteria but CONTACT:

- The ball (steel, 1000 m/s) passes through the plate: it keeps 983 m/s and the plate absorbs about 1% of its energy.
- Up to 483 ball nodes sit inside LIVE ghost elements at once; 643 distinct live ghosts were entered by a ball node. All 58 "islands" (ghosts alive at the end that should have been deleted) were entered while alive.
- Only 285 of 27,401 plate nodes ever show CPRESS > 0, mostly for a single output frame, with spikes of 4e10 to 6e11 Pa. The contact is impulsive and weak.
- I reconstructed the VU1 failure test from nodal displacements for all 24,000 hosts (8 Gauss points, Green-Lagrange normal strain, same as `source/VUEL_failure.for`). It predicts 1364 VU1 failures; the solver printed exactly 1364. So the reconstruction is validated (`evidence/el_elements.csv`, one row per host).
- 74 of the 85 surviving ghosts within 10 mm of the axis have an intact VU1 too: nothing to follow. Only 104 hosts plate-wide have a failed VU1 with a live ghost.

## Scheme (so you do not have to rediscover it)

`embmesh/vuelghost.py` turns each host hex into TWO elements on the SAME node labels: a `VU1` user element (the real stiffness and mass, with its own failure trigger; it cannot be deleted by Abaqus and cannot take part in contact) and a native `C3D8R` ghost (label = host + 24000) whose material is the host material scaled by 1e-4 (E 508 Pa, density 0.098, yield 61.1 Pa), with `*Damage Initiation, criterion=DUCTILE` at PEEQ 1e-6 and `*Damage Evolution, type=DISPLACEMENT` 0, so it deletes the instant it yields. The ghost exists only so the failed region vanishes and the surface can take part in contact. Host matrix: E 5.08 MPa, density 980, yield 611 kPa, very soft. Ball: mild steel, Johnson-Cook with damage, 1000 m/s. Explicit, nlgeom, 7e-5 s, general contact (hard, friction 0.01) between element-based surfaces `Surf-1` (plate original exterior faces, remapped to ghost labels by the mesher) and `Surf-2` (ball). The user deck is `evidence/Flatv2_user.inp`, the mesher output is `evidence/flat_v2_output.inp` (14 MB; search `*Contact`, `EMBMESH_GHOST_MATERIAL`, `*Surface`).

## Hypotheses to rank (none tested)

H1. Penalty stiffness is derived from the surface elements, which are the 1e-4-scaled ghosts, so contact is far too soft. The VU1 mass and stiffness are on the same nodes but are not part of the surface.
H2. The element-based surface contains only the ORIGINAL exterior faces, so faces exposed by deletion never join the surface (compare `ALL EXTERIOR`, which does adapt, though earlier `ALL EXTERIOR` runs also showed islands).
H3. Steel against a 5 MPa matrix at 1000 m/s: a stiffness and mass mismatch the default explicit penalty cannot resolve in one increment.
H4. Something else you find in the data.
