NewVals NV3 Contact Evolution: Contact Overclosure Pathology, Eroding Element Contact, and Interior Faces

Author: Drafter (T-060) · Date: 2026-11-06 · Decks: Meshing/newvals-*/ · ROAR directory: ctv/bt3/nv3_*

Discovery of the Contact Thickness Overclosure Pathology:
When advancing to the 3-elements-per-ply architecture (NV3, 0.423 mm hex thickness), the initial deck nv3_smear suffered catastrophic failure: 27,293 host elements deleted in increment 1, before the ball ever reached the plate! Investigation revealed that the mesher option --plate-node-contact assigned an automatic contact thickness of 0.2117 mm (half host edge) to all ply nodes. Across cohesive inter-ply interfaces, this artificial thickness caused severe geometric overclosure, triggering immediate matrix crushing prior to projectile impact (t ≤ 0.97 μs).

1. Overview & Architectural Progression

The NewVals NV3 series represents the production meshing architecture for the 25.4 mm composite plate: 20 plies with 3 hex elements through the thickness of each ply (435,000 host hexes, 524,180 fiber trusses), paper Table 2 material cards, and the corrected 3/8-inch rigid sphere at 1028 m/s. 4. Interior Face Contact Check (nv3_int)

To prepare for complex inter-ply debris interaction during perforation, nv3_int implemented an eroding contact surface including both EXTERIOR and INTERIOR faces of ghost host elements (*Contact Controls Assignment, nodal erosion=YES). Key findings from the 2 μs ROAR test (job 57005555):

  • Datacheck confirmed that nodal erosion=YES accepts no trailing data lines (fixing an initial syntax abort).
  • Zero pre-impact deletions and zero contact overclosure spikes.
  • ETOTAL stayed perfectly flat: 469.2 J → 468.0 J (-0.25%) over 2.0 μs.
  • Run completed in 8 min 43 s wall time, proving high efficiency for early impact phases.

5. Findings & Next Steps

  • Element-Based Contact Eliminates False Failures: Switching from node contact to element contact surfaces completely eliminated the 27,000+ spurious pre-impact host failures.
  • 100 μs Viability: nv3_elem demonstrated excellent numerical stability, maintaining a 38 ns time step and completing 100 μs in 13.3 hours without requiring restart recovery.
  • Low Fiber Failure: Only 73 fibers deleted out of 524,180, indicating that embedded fibers primarily deform rather than snap under 1000 m/s ball impact.

6. What Changed Next

While plate-ball contact was stabilized, the question arose whether fibers within each ply require contact with surrounding matrix elements as those hosts erode. This prompted T-056 and the fiber-host contact investigation documented in FIBER_HOST_CONTACT_REPORT.html.
